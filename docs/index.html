Episode triage

A tool that reads robot episode logs, finds where the robot stopped making progress or did something wrong, works out whether a camera, a joint, or the task started it, and puts each event on a ladder: clean, the robot can clear it itself, a ticket for a named part, send it to training, or a person has to look.

Independent project by Avi Desai. Not affiliated with Weave Robotics. Data: the LeRobot community dataset (Apache 2.0), 1,427 SO 100 and SO 101 arms built and calibrated by different people. Code and every number below: github.com/avidesai/episode-triage.

All 48,208 of 48,208 episodes have a perfectly regular timestamp column, because the recorder writes frame_index / fps instead of a measured time, so no timing check on the clock can find anything. Reading each camera's pictures against the joints that move it, 58 of 809 sampled episodes from 43 robots had a camera that stopped updating while the arm was moving.

Before running it I predicted that at least a quarter of the stalls and missed grasp sequences would turn out to start with a sensing or actuation fault. It was 4 of 355: in this teleoperated data nearly every stall is a person pausing, with fresh pictures and joints that track. What the cross check buys is that those 322 now go to training with the other layers checked rather than assumed. The faults it does find sit below the task: across all 1,427 robots, 197 carry a joint level fault worth a ticket, mostly calibration.

The ladder

Ordered rules, first match wins. Camera to joint lag

The delay at which a camera's image change best lines up with the motion of the joints it sees, as a median over each robot's sampled episodes (one episode's estimate moves by a frame, so this is judged per robot). Most of the 395 camera streams sit at 0 to 2 frames. 46 streams on 40 robots trail their joints by 3 frames (100 ms) or more, up to 5. Merged for training, those datasets disagree about when the gripper saw the object. 4 streams estimate the camera ahead of the joints, which shouldn't happen and most likely means the estimate is wrong for them.

Does it find what it says it finds?

Faults were injected into real community episodes with a known answer. Video faults were reencoded with LeRobot's own defaults (AV1, crf 30, GOP 2), so a frozen frame reaches the detector through the same compression a real one would. One threshold was tuned on data: the image change level for a frozen frame, set to 0.51 gray levels on 20 calibration robots. Everything was then frozen and scored on different robots. "Low conf." means the detector found the frozen run but the camera barely shows the arm moving, so the run is logged and the ladder doesn't act on it; that guard is what keeps a camera pointed away from the arm from being blamed for a stall, and it costs recall on more than half these episodes. "Abstained" means the episode had fewer than two rest periods, so a resting offset couldn't be measured. A freeze scored at rung 3 started after a stall, so it was correctly context. Camera delayed by 3 frames: the lag estimate moved by 3 plus or minus 1 on 37 of 38 episodes where both estimates existed (40 tried). "Other new events" counts anything else the injection made fire. Injected faults test the detectors and the ladder; they don't test whether natural faults look the same.

What a robot should record so this stops being inference

  • Capture time per frame, from the camera or a hardware trigger, next to the loop time. With those, stale frames and camera lag become a subtraction instead of a cross check.
  • Real loop period. A slow loop is only a log line today; nothing about it reaches the data.
  • Per motor load, temperature, voltage and status byte. The servos on these arms report all four over the same bus and none is recorded by default. They would turn "not following" into "unloaded on overload" or "brownout".
  • The goal actually sent after any clipping, beside the leader or policy command.
  • Every takeover with the progress score and subsystem health at that moment. Each one is a labeled anchor.

The flight recorder in recorder/ logs the first four on an SO 101 as a sidecar to a normal LeRobot dataset.

What's wrong with it

  • Stale camera and lag are inferred from picture content against joint motion, because the data has no real clock. A camera pointed at a blank surface while moving can look frozen; those runs get low confidence and stay off the counts.
  • "Policy" in teleop data means the operator. Rung 3 here is task level stalls and misses, not a learned policy failing.
  • Proprioception alone can't always tell a thin cloth grasp from an empty close.
  • Each dataset counts as one robot. Some people recorded several datasets on one arm, so robot counts are an upper bound.
  • The video checks ran on a seeded sample (298 robots, up to 3 episodes each), not every episode.
  • Injected faults are cleaner than natural ones.
